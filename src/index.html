IO Controls / Setup & folder structure

Setup & the folder structure

What a new starter's Workspace looks like on day one, and the three steps to get from an empty folder to a working assistant.

01 First session

  1. 1Choose where it livesPut the Workspace folder somewhere permanent — not somewhere it might move, and not a OneDrive-synced folder. Setup writes this exact location into its own files.
  2. 2Open it in Claude CodeNot the Claude chat app — this Workspace is built to run in Claude Code specifically. See module 05 for the difference.
  3. 3Type /setupAbout fifteen minutes. Claude asks who you are, interviews you for your context files, and — with your permission, asked separately — makes two small changes outside the folder.

02 What you receive: the Workspace folder

Everything lives inside one folder, called Workspace. You open this folder in Claude Code — nothing else. Inside it, at the top level, are two files and four folders:

Workspace/ ├── 00 START HERE.md ├── CLAUDE.md ├── 01 Context/ ├── 02 Projects/ ├── 03 Archive/ └── .claude/
?
What's an .md file?

Files ending in .md (short for "Markdown") are plain text files with simple marks for headings and lists — no special software needed, Notepad opens them fine. Claude reads them and turns them into properly formatted answers.

ItemWhat it is
00 START HERE.mdThe very first thing to open. A plain-English map of the whole folder, written for you, not for Claude.
CLAUDE.mdAlso a .md file, but this one is for Claude, not you. It reads it at the start of every session: who you are, what rules to follow, where everything is.
01 Context folderWhat Claude knows about you: your role, your priorities, the people you work with, decisions that have been made, and a Reference folder for background material — including this training.
02 Projects folderEverywhere your work lives, sorted into four folders: Automations, Knowledge Bases, Artefacts, Apps. Module 04 covers this in full.
03 Archive folderOld work brought in from before you had this Workspace, kept exactly as it arrived. Read only — bring things in with /archive-intake, work on a copy with /migrate-project. See Module 03.
.claude folderThe engine room: the rules and skills that make Claude behave consistently. You never need to open this.

03 What CLAUDE.md actually tells Claude

CLAUDE.md is the file Claude reads first, every single session. Two things in it are worth knowing about, because they explain why Claude behaves the way it does.

There are two kinds of session. Open your main Workspace folder, and Claude acts as your assistant generally — your context, your diary, your people, any of your projects. Open a project's own folder instead (somewhere under 02 Projects\), and Claude works specifically on that project, reading its own README and rules as well. Either way, Claude still knows who you are — your context files are the same.

It lists six rules to follow, every session:

Rule fileWhat it governs
communication-style.mdHow Claude writes to you, and on your behalf — tone, length, what to avoid.
accuracy.mdNever invent facts. Say where information came from. Call out priorities rather than letting things drift.
technical-writing.mdPlain, unambiguous English for anything technical — procedures, work instructions, engineering reports.
microsoft-365.mdHow the Microsoft 365 connector is used, and the drafts-never-send rule from Module 06.
design-system.mdWhich of the two brand systems applies — internal material versus the public website.
workspace.mdHow this folder is organised, and how Claude keeps it that way as it grows.

None of these need reading in full — they're what make Claude consistent for everyone, whoever opens the folder.

04 Words you will see

WordMeans
SkillA saved way of doing a job. Run one by typing / and its name.
RuleA standing instruction Claude follows in every session — the six listed above.
ContextThe files in 01 Context\. What Claude knows about you.
MemorySmall things Claude noted for itself between sessions. Lives in .claude\memory\.

None of the six training modules are a step in setup, and nobody is walked through them during onboarding. They are here from day one, ready whenever they're wanted.

Getting started / 01 — Welcome

Welcome, and how this all fits together

What Workspace is, why every person gets their own copy, and the one-line mental model for how it works.

Getting started2 min read

01 What Workspace is

A folder on your machine, opened in Claude Code, set up to work as your executive assistant. It holds:

  • What Claude knows about you — your context files
  • Your work — organised into projects
  • Old work — brought in and frozen in the archive
  • The rules and skills underneath — never opened by hand

02 Why everyone gets their own copy

Everyone works differently and knows different things, whatever their role. Your Workspace holds your own priorities, your own people, your own projects. It is not a shared company database. Your Microsoft 365 connection is your own login — your Workspace does not see anyone else's mailbox, calendar or files, and no one else sees yours.

What is shared is the structure: the same folders, the same rules, the same skills, the same four project types, the same brand system.

03 The one-line mental model

Every session, Claude reads your Workspace's CLAUDE.md first. That file tells it who you are, points it at your context files, and lists the rules to follow. Everything else follows from that one read.

Whoever does a job, it gets done the same way — that is the whole point of everyone having the same structure.

Getting started / 02 — Getting set up

Getting set up

First session steps, running /setup, connecting Microsoft 365, and the optional Graph PowerShell step.

Getting started3 min read

01 Before you start

Put your Workspace folder somewhere permanent — not somewhere that might move, or get synced by OneDrive. Some of the setup writes the folder's exact location into its own files.

02 Running /setup

  1. Who you are — name, role, work email, timezone.
  2. The test — one line on what a good assistant does for you.
  3. The interview — day-to-day work, priorities, background. Short version available.
  4. Style — how you like to be written to, and a sign-off for drafts.
  5. Two machine-level changes, asked about separately.

/setup is safe to run again later — it only fills gaps, it never overwrites an answer you already gave.

03 Connecting Microsoft 365

Read-only access to your own Outlook, Calendar, Teams and SharePoint. You connect it yourself in the Claude app's own connector settings — /setup can point you there, but cannot do it for you. Once connected, Claude can read but cannot send, post or change anything without your approval. Anything drafted on your behalf stops at a draft — a human sends it.

Reference / 04 — Starting a new project

Starting a new project

The four project types, and how to create, switch and keep working on one.

Reference4 min read

01 The four types

TypeUse it when
AutomationA repeating job. Usually ends as a skill.
Knowledge BaseGrows and gets corrected. Never finished.
ArtefactOne finished thing for one audience. Then stops.
AppSoftware people keep using. The code is the product.

02 Does Claude decide the type for you?

Not on its own. When you describe what you want, Claude recommends a type and says why — but you can just tell it the type yourself instead. Only when it is genuinely unclear does Claude make the call, using a simple rule: what is the actual end result? A file for someone → Artefact. Something that repeats → Automation. A growing body of knowledge → Knowledge Base. Code people run → App.

03 How to create a project

  1. Open your Workspace folder in Claude Code (or a project folder, if you're already inside one).
  2. Type /new-project, or just describe what you want to build.
  3. Give it a name in plain words, and one sentence on the purpose.
  4. Claude builds the skeleton and adds a row to the Projects Index.

04 How "saving" works

There is no separate save step. Every file Claude writes is saved to disk the moment it is written — nothing sits unsaved, waiting to be lost. What you do want to do at the end of a session is run /wrap-up: not to save files, but to log what happened — decisions made, status changed, priorities updated — so the next session picks up where you left off.

05 How to switch which project you're working on

Two ways, both equally valid:

  • Stay in your main Workspace folder and tell Claude which project you want to work on by name — it opens that project's own README and works inside it.
  • Or open that project's own folder directly in Claude Code, under 02 Projects\. Claude still knows who you are either way — your context files are the same.

A project not in the Projects Index does not exist, as far as your Workspace is concerned.

When to use Claude vs Claude Code

The practical difference between Claude chat and Claude Code, and which one to reach for.

Reference2 min read

01 Two different tools

Claude — the chat appClaude Code
A conversation. Good for a quick question, thinking something through, a short draft.The tool your Workspace runs on. Opens a folder, reads and writes files directly.

Reference / 06 — Day-to-day best practice

Day-to-day best practice

The habits that keep a Workspace useful over time.

Reference2 min read

01 Skills vs one-off chat

One-off, just ask. Third time round, worth becoming a skill.

02 Drafts never send

Email, Teams, anything client facing — stops at a draft. A human approves before it leaves. Not a setting you can turn off.

03 Wrap-up discipline

Run /wrap-up at the end of a session. It updates the project, logs decisions, updates priorities and commitments, and gives you a short report.

04 What memory is, and is not, for

Memory: small working facts — a tool quirk, a preference, a hard-won path.
Not memory: durable facts about you, the business or a person → 01 Context. Facts about a project → that project's own README.

A contradiction gets written as "now X, previously Y" — never silently overwritten.

Reference / 07 — Claude, the absolute basics

Claude, the absolute basics

Not about your Workspace — about Claude itself. Tabs, opening a folder, seeing files, switching conversations, and the settings worth knowing early.

Reference3 min read
?
Checked against the real app, not just documentation

An earlier draft of this module relied on Claude Code's written documentation alone, and one section turned out to be flatly wrong — the documentation didn't even mention the file tree panel below. Everything here has since been checked against the actual Claude desktop app. Worth a second look yourself if anything seems off, since app details can still change between versions.

01 Three tabs, three different things

Claude's desktop app has three tabs, and mixing them up is the most common beginner confusion.

TabWhat it isUse it for
ChatA conversation, like claude.ai. No access to your files.Quick questions, drafting, thinking something through.
CoworkAn agent that runs in the background, in its own sandboxed space, separate from what you're doing.Longer, independent jobs you kick off and check back on later.
CodeClaude working directly inside a folder on your machine — reads and writes files, runs skills.Everything in this training. Your Workspace lives here.

Everything in this training is about the Code tab specifically. When something says "open Claude Code," it means: open the desktop app, click Code.

02 Opening your Workspace folder

  1. Open the Claude desktop app.
  2. Click the Code tab.
  3. Click Select folder and choose your Workspace folder.
  4. If asked whether to run it locally or in the cloud, choose local.

03 Seeing what files you actually have

Claude Code has a file tree panel — folders you can expand, a filter box to search by filename, the same structure you'd see in Windows Explorer.

  1. Click the menu icon, top left (☰).
  2. Choose View.
  3. Choose Files.

You can also just ask: "what files are in this folder?" — Claude will tell you. Or open the folder in Windows Explorer, exactly as you would any other folder, separately from Claude entirely.

04 Switching between conversations

If you have more than one conversation open — say, one for your main Workspace and another for a specific project — they show as a list in a sidebar. Click one to switch to it. There are also keyboard shortcuts to cycle between them.

05 Settings worth knowing early

You do not need to learn every setting Claude has. A handful are worth knowing from day one:

SettingWhat it does
Permission modeHow much Claude can do without asking you first — from asking before every change, up to handling routine things itself.
ModelWhich underlying Claude model is doing the work. Affects speed and capability.
ThemeLight or dark mode. Convenience only.

Everything else — environment variables, advanced project settings, anything called "managed" or organisation-level — is not a day-one concern. Ignore it until you have a specific reason to look.

That's genuinely the basics. Everything else in this training assumes you can now open your Workspace, see its files, and find your way back to a conversation you had before.
